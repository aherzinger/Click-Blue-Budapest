# Click-Blue-Budapest

> A community space on the Danube, in the heart of Budapest, where people reconnect with the river through sport, learning and science.

---

## 1. The Problem

- **It is not possible to do watersports on the Danube** in Budapest today.
- Budapest **lacks community spaces / areas** where people can meet and connect.
- Citizens have lost their relationship with the river: the Danube is seen only as something polluted, not as something to live.

---

## 2. Goal

**Raise awareness**, not only about the importance of taking care of water pollution, **but about the fact that we can actually live the water as we used to.**

---

## 3. The Idea

Create a **watersports area / community space on the Danube, in the city centre**, where you can:

- **Try new sports with experts** – learn something new that could become a new passion.
- **Join seminars, workshops, events and hackathons** to raise awareness among Budapest's citizens.
- **Take part in scientific experiments** to monitor the Danube's pollution.

### Location

- **City centre**: accessible and visible to everyone walking in the city, including tourists.
- **Close to the spot where the water is warm**, so people can swim in winter as well.

---

## 4. Practical Activities

| Activity | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sports days / events** | People learn something new, which could become a new passion. |
| **Workshops & seminars** | Educate citizens about the river and its ecosystem. |
| **Specific hackathons** | Hosted in specific regions/cities to solve their specific needs. |
| **Scientific experiments** | Monitor the Danube's pollution, involving the community. |

---

## 5. Why It Works

- **Feel part of something.** Only after people actually realise the importance of the river are they more likely to take action.
- **Sport connects people**, and it also connects **people with nature** (in this case, the river).
- It **builds communities and awareness**.
- It **educates** through workshops and events.
- It **creates new blue jobs**: instructors, teachers, equipment rental, etc.

---

## 6. Theory of Change: Connection → Wellbeing → Care

> "We will conserve only what we love." – Baba Dioum

The project does not start from awareness campaigns. It starts from **experience**:

1. **Connection** – people come back to the river through sport, events and community.
2. **Wellbeing** – they start associating the Danube with feeling good (the "blue space" effect on stress and mental health).
3. **Attachment** – repeated rituals (weekly sessions, winter swimming, sauna) turn the river into *"my place"*.
4. **Care** – people protect what they feel is theirs. Taking care of the river becomes a natural consequence, not an obligation.

How we make it happen:

- **Wellbeing first**: pitch the space as a public health and quality-of-life asset, not just a sports facility.
- **Rituals, not one-off visits**: regular formats that build habits and belonging.
- **A role, not just a ticket**: regular users take part in water sampling and pollution monitoring, with results made public.
- **Measure the shift**: use the interviews as a baseline on how people feel about the Danube, then survey users again after a season.

---

## 7. Modular Build Plan

The space is built **step by step with standardized floating modules**. Each module has one function, connects to the others, and can be added, moved or replaced. This makes the project feasible, lowers risk, and is itself part of the innovation.

### The upstream module: trash collector

The **first module, the one facing upstream and receiving the current straight on**, will integrate a **floating trash collector**:

- It intercepts floating waste before it reaches the rest of the space and continues downstream.
- It makes pollution **visible**: the collected waste can be shown, weighed and communicated, turning an abstract problem into something people see every day.
- It produces **data** on the type and amount of waste, feeding the citizen-science monitoring program.
- It sends a clear message from day one: the space doesn't only use the river, it gives something back.

References to research: Mr. Trash Wheel (Baltimore), Seabin, The Great Bubble Barrier (Amsterdam).

### Phases

| Phase | What we add | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **0 – Pop-up** | Sports days and events on existing riverbank spots, borrowed equipment | Test demand, build the first community at almost no cost |
| **1 – Core module** | Upstream module with **trash collector** + floating deck with storage, changing space, SUP and kayak rental with instructors | First blue jobs, first daily users, visible environmental action |
| **2 – Wellbeing modules** | Sauna module, then a protected swimming area (where water quality and permits allow) | Build rituals and the wellbeing association, including winter use |
| **3 – Knowledge modules** | Lab / education module, event deck | Monitoring program, workshops, seminars and hackathons |

### Why modular

- **Financing**: each module can have its own sponsor (Tallinn-airport-style sponsor model).
- **Risk**: if one phase is delayed by permits, the rest keeps running.
- **Swimming**: start with paddle sports and add swimming later, backed by our own monitoring data.
- **Future-proof**: modules can be reconfigured as the river and the city change.
- **Replicable**: the same kit can be adopted by other Danube cities, linked to region-specific hackathons.

## 8. Impact

Map the project against the three impact types:

- **Economic** – new jobs (teaching, renting equipment), tourism, sponsorship opportunities.
- **Social** – a new community space for citizens, reconnecting people with the Danube.
- **Environmental** – trash collector intercepting floating waste, awareness about pollution, citizen science to monitor the river's health.

> **Tip:** Plan ahead with a long-term view. **Make it future-proof.**
